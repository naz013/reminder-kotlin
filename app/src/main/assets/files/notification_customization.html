How Notification Customization Works

Vibration, sound, priority, and more — set once, override wherever you need

Key Concept

Every notification setting (vibration, vibration pattern, repeating, delay, priority, category, bypassing Do Not Disturb, waking the screen, lock screen visibility) can be set at three levels. The most specific level you've customized always wins.

The Three Levels

1

Settings BASE

Your app-wide defaults, found in Settings → Reminders → Notification defaults. Every value here is always set — this is what applies unless you override it somewhere more specific.

2

Group OPTIONAL

Per-group overrides, found when editing a group under Notification overrides. Leave a field on "Inherit from Settings" and it falls back to your app-wide default; override it and every reminder in that group picks it up.

3

Reminder MOST SPECIFIC

Per-reminder overrides, set directly in the reminder builder. These take priority over everything else — perfect for the one reminder that needs to be louder, quieter, or silent, without changing the rest of its group.

If you set it on......it applies to
ReminderJust that one reminder
GroupEvery reminder in that group that hasn't overridden it itself
SettingsEvery reminder that hasn't overridden it at the group or reminder level

What You Can Customize

Vibrate

Turn vibration on or off for the notification.

Vibration Pattern

Pick a pattern (Short, Long, Double buzz, Default) so different reminders feel different.

Repeating

Whether the notification keeps repeating until you act on it.

Notification Delay

Delay the notification by a number of minutes.

Priority

How prominently the notification is shown.

Category

Tags the notification as Default, Alarm, Event, or Call, which affects how your device treats it.

Bypass Do Not Disturb

Let this notification ring even while Do Not Disturb is on.

Wake Screen

Turn the screen on like an alarm when the notification fires.

Lock Screen Visibility

Show full content, hide sensitive content, or hide the notification completely on the lock screen.

Example

Say your Settings default has vibration on and Normal priority. You create a "Work" group and turn its priority to High. Most work reminders now fire at High priority automatically. For one especially important meeting, you open that single reminder and turn on "Bypass Do Not Disturb" — only that reminder rings through Do Not Disturb; the rest of the group is unaffected.

Device and Android version differences

Not every option behaves the same way on every device or Android version. Some manufacturers restrict vibration patterns, custom sounds, or bypassing Do Not Disturb; battery-optimization or "deep sleep" modes on some phones can delay notifications regardless of what you've set; and once a notification channel is created, Android itself may lock its sound and vibration behavior in the system notification settings for that channel. If something doesn't behave as expected, check your device's own notification/app settings alongside what's configured here.

Reminder wins, then Group, then Settings.
